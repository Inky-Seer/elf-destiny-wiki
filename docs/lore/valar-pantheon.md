# The Valar Pantheon

The **Valar** are the gods of the elves — and the most important truth about them is
that they were not born divine. Every Vala is an **ascended elf**: an individual who
climbed the [Ascension](elven-ascension.md) ladder all the way to its summit through
their own power and their service to the [Divine Spark](divine-spark-and-cosmology.md).

They are not a separate order of creation standing above mortals. They are the
current membership of a club that is, in principle, open to anyone who can climb
high enough. The present pantheon simply happens to be the only elves who ever have.

The Valar are also not all of one kind. A variety of elven races are represented
among them, some forming their own smaller pantheons and worshipping in their own
ways — yet all still answer to the Divine Spark.

## The pantheon

| Vala | Domain |
|---|---|
| **Manwë Súlimo** | King of the Winds, and the chief of the Valar — remembered as the *First Emperor*. |
| **Ulmo** | The waters and the seas. |
| **Aulë** | Earth, craft, and wealth — the great Smith. |
| **Yavanna Kementári** | Nature, growth, and the prosperity of the household. |
| **Varda** | The night and the stars. |
| **Vána** | Beauty and fertility. |
| **Estë** | Healing and rest. |
| **Vairë** | Fate, and the weaving of time. |
| **Irmo** | Dreams, visions, and knowledge. |
| **Tulkas Astaldo** | War and strength. |
| **Nessa** | Dance and celebration. |
| **Mandos** | Death — the judge of the dead. |
| **Oromë** | The hunt and the deep forests. |
| **Nienna** | Grief and mercy. |

That **Manwë Súlimo** is titled the *First Emperor* is no small detail: it binds the
chief of the gods directly to the imperial history of the elves and the founding of
their civilisation. The pantheon is said to number fourteen, and elven lore still
speaks of Valar yet to be named.

## Melkor, the Fallen Vala

Not every Vala remains true. **Melkor** is the dark god of the pantheon — the God of
the Void and the First Dark Lord. He is proof of the pantheon's most unsettling
possibility: that a being can Ascend to the very heights of godhood and then turn
against the purpose of the Divine Spark itself. A god, once made, can still fall.

## The Maia

Below the Valar stand the **Maia** — demi-gods and servants of the Valar, the lowest
rank of the divine Ainur. Elven legend holds that some of the world's famed wise
ones walk among mortals as Maia in disguise, their true nature hidden.

## Becoming a god

The Valar are the living proof of where Ascension leads. The three highest tiers —
**Maia**, **Vala**, and **Aratar** — are the divine Ainur, and an elf who Ascends
that far does not merely come to *resemble* a god. They **become** one. The pantheon
is not a closed circle; it is the far end of a road any elf may, in principle, walk.

## The Valar in play

The Divine Spark grants **blessings** in the names of individual Valar, bestowed
through ritual upon a whole elven people:

- **Blessing of Tulkas Astaldo** — the war-god's gift, strengthening a people for
  battle.
- **Blessing of Vána** — the gift of the goddess of fertility and prosperity.

Each Vala embodies a different facet of the world, and their blessings carry
something of that domain to the elves who earn them.
