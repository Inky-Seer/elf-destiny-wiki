# Dark Elves

Across the history of the world, one shadow falls again and again: the **Dark
Elves**. They are not a separate species — they are elves who turned to **blood
magic and forbidden arts**, defined not by their blood but by their practices: blood
sacrifice, the forging of twisted creatures, cannibalism, and the pursuit of power
by any means at all.

## Not simply evil

It would be easy to call the Dark Elves monsters and leave it there — but the lore
refuses the simple answer. They have their own traditions and their own complexity,
and not all of them stood with the worst of their kind.

The clearest proof is the fall of the Dark Lord **Veldric**: when that mad warlock
had to be stopped, the alliance that brought down his tower was *"an unlikely
collaboration of High and Dark Elves"*. Some Dark Elves turned against their own to
fight beside the High Elves. The line between dark and light has always run through
the elves, not neatly between them.

## Dark lords of history

- **Queen Hekla** — a sorceress queen of immense power who raised her **Black
  Palace** into the side of a volcano and drew on its fire to fuel her greatest
  spells. She led legions of Dark Elves in conquest until the **Light's Champions**,
  the mightiest magi and Empowered of the northern elf kingdoms, gave their lives to
  destroy her.
- **The Dark Lord Veldric** — *"few are as vile,"* the histories say. His lust for
  power was matched only by his ability, and it took five of the greatest mages of
  the age — High and Dark Elves together — to bring his **Shattered Spire** down on
  his head.
- **Aji Saka, the Cannibal King** — a Dark Elven king who devoured his victims alive
  in his quest for power, until an elven adventurer cast him from the highest cliff
  of his fortress into the sea. His body was never found, and his ruin is now called
  *Cannibal's End*.

## Vael'Drakhor, the Blood Forge

The greatest Dark Elven power was **Vael'Drakhor** — their capital, remembered in old
texts as **the Blood Forge**.

> *Here, countless bloodmages of unmatched skill practised their forbidden arts,
> forging twisted creatures for their armies — horrors known and feared across the
> world. Their empire was immensely powerful, yet its downfall is believed to have
> come not from invaders, but from a cataclysm of their own making.*

Vael'Drakhor's fall is the same grim pattern that runs through all of elven history:
not conquest from without, but **magical hubris from within**. Its ruins are said to
be guarded still by giant, venom-spraying death worms beneath the sands.

## The Grand Blood Altar

At the **Grand Blood Altar**, the Dark Elves sacrificed thousands in pursuit of a
fabled **blood stone** — a relic that would make its wielder all but unchallengeable,
short of the gods themselves. One of the greatest battles in history was fought to
stop them. They were stopped — but how far their research had already progressed,
the lore leaves chillingly unanswered.

## A world-wide shadow

The reach of the Dark Elves was never confined to one land. A cruel dark elven
emperor's betrayal triggered the catastrophe of Meng Jiang's Wrath; a Dark Elven
invasion drove the warg-lord Hwanung to wake a volcano against them. Wherever elven
history runs dark, the Dark Elves are rarely far away.

!!! note "What the future may hold"
    The Dark Elves stand, for now, as the great antagonists of elven *history*. Lore
    points toward a future in which they become a playable people in their own
    right, with their own religion, culture, and dark traditions — and perhaps a
    connection to the [rival creator gods](divine-spark-and-cosmology.md) who covet
    this world.
